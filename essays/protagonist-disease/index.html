Protagonist Disease

by Aryan Pahwani

Protagonist disease refers to thinking the world revolves around you in a grand narrative. This means you’re constantly expecting a grand revelation, a life-changing moment, or an underdog arc, romanticising your life and not making the smart decisions you could otherwise be making.

This doesn’t mean you’re narcissistic. You could genuinely care about other people, just in a way where you’re their saviour, or automatically extremely important in others’ lives. Narcissism is much more about the motive. A narcissistic individual cares only about themselves, but knows that others may not.

Narcissism can be morally reprehensible, but you can still be clear-eyed and make good decisions. With protagonist disease, you will continuously ignore key information coming your way, or interpret it only in last principles which benefit the story.

Protagonist disease reduces your contact with reality and makes it hard to read other people. Setbacks and even catastrophic mistakes are read within the story, so you rarely learn from them. Any error reads as some grand harm from the universe done to you, which makes you spiral harder and harder. You overvalue dramatic moves rather than boring work that doesn’t bring in attention.

Your life is not going to change in one day, and you should stop trying to make it so. Your life will change through a multitude of smart decisions.

Main characters live stories; side characters get shit done.
